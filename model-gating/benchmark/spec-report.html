ReproCrowd benchmark: what it measures and how it runs

Specification draft 0.4 · 1 November 2026 · agreed with GPT-6.1 Sol and Gemini 4 Argon · full specification

The benchmark tests whether a model can identify a paper's key claims, reproduce them and judge them correctly. It has two tracks: with the authors' code, and blinded without it. Each track gets one end-to-end score. A claim earns credit only when the model shows the computation behind it, not just the right label. Each stage reads and writes fixed file formats, so any model, prompt or sequence of steps can be swapped in and scored the same way. After three review rounds, Sol and Argon both agree with draft 0.4 as written. Seven of its rules are new choices that you have not seen yet.

Needs your decision

before freezing1. Seven rules that Claude, Sol and Argon agreed, not yet seen by you

  1. Variation first. If an analysis is not exactly reproducible (for example an unseeded bootstrap), its result gets within_variation, even when one run happens to print the reported value. The aim is to reward noticing nondeterminism consistently. If a model recovers the original seed and versions, the normal rules apply and earn credit too.
  2. Paper outcome.
    • Computationally reproducible: every assessed result is match, close or within_variation. Reporting slips such as typos are listed, but the computation does not count as failed.
    • Technical failure: blockers prevent every primary computation.
    • Computational issues: anything in between.
  3. Paper outcome in the headline. A wrong paper outcome halves that paper's score.
  4. Fabrication gate. Two or more values that the model says the paper prints, but which it does not, give the paper a score of 0. One instance is recorded but not penalised.
  5. Evidence for a reporting slip must be in the package. Models may not read reproduction reports, so a typo that only a human report identifies cannot justify close, for the model or in gold. The evidence must be:
    • an erratum in the package;
    • a donor value visible in the executed output; or
    • a single-character slip whose companion statistics match.
    Consequence: G-22 C2 and C3 become discrepant, because the intercept and its interval differ together. Sol checked the published table and the pinned output.
  6. Rendered outputs removed from every package, not just twins. Otherwise a package without outputs would give a twin away, and a model could copy values from shipped tables instead of computing them. Code that exists only inside a rendered document (as in G-22) is extracted first.
  7. At most 15 claims per certificate. Without a cap, listing every number in a paper would reach full recall without any selection. Gold has 5 to 8 claims per paper, and 15 is a judgement, not a calibrated figure.

Recommendation: accept all seven. The ones with visible consequences are rules 2 and 5. Under rule 2, G-07 and G-15 may move to computationally reproducible, if checks of their companion statistics and donor row confirm the slips. Under rule 5, G-22 gains two discrepant claims; its paper outcome stays computational issues.

Decisions to review

  • Stata goes to R only. You left R or Python to me. One target language keeps translations comparable.
  • No numerical ceiling on close. The old rule allowed a gap of up to 1 unit or 1%. Now close needs a demonstrated slip (truncation, transposed digits, a copied cell, or a typo evidenced inside the package), and undoing it must give an exact match. This answers your "why more?": rounding is the only tolerance for match, and close rests on evidence rather than size.
  • One label per gold result. Gold v1.4 drops the lists of acceptable alternative labels. A case the labellers cannot settle is held instead.
  • Print statements are an allowed fix. A model may add lines that only print values (for example the unrounded estimate behind a rounded table), so that it can show the computed number.
  • "Held" gold results. If the evidence does not yet support a label, the result is excluded from scoring until reruns settle it. It is not called not_checkable, because that would describe the study rather than our evidence.
  • Claim identification is recall of a must-check subset. The gold lists (5 to 8 claims per paper) are the claims the human sources assessed, not every important claim. The benchmark claims no more than that. Extra claims are audited, not penalised.
  • Track B gold is built independently by two labellers working only from the article and data, not copied from Track A. The published method and the shipped code can differ, as G-21 shows.
  • Twins count as one target each in the headline, but uncertainty intervals resample each twin together with its base paper.
  • Some reviewer suggestions were not adopted.
    • Accepting both labels for G-21 (Argon, round 1): this would reopen your decision.
    • Accepting match for G-16 because the shipped tables agree with the paper (Argon, round 1): reading shipped output is not recomputation.
    • A small relative tolerance against gold references (Argon, round 2): rounding both values to the printed precision is enough.
    • A cap on results (Argon, round 2): some claims legitimately have many results, such as G-27 with six per claim.
    Both reviewers accepted these decisions.

Targets: the 30 gold papers and 8 seeded twins (Track A), about 10 Stata papers to be added, and the claims in each paper that are eligible for Track B. Twins contain a planted error in the code, so they test Track A only.

Claim statuses

StatusWhen
matchRounding the recomputed value to the printed precision gives the printed value, by round-half-up or round-half-to-even. That is the only tolerance.
closeA deterministic analysis where the printed value differs because of one reporting slip demonstrated from the package and the run, and undoing the slip gives a match. A flipped sign, a change in whether an interval contains the null, a false printed bound or a crossed threshold always counts as discrepant. Counts must be exact.
within_variationThe analysis is not exactly reproducible, and the printed value lies within the range of repeated runs. The causes are an unseeded random process, a seed whose random stream changed between versions, a package version that cannot be recovered, parallel order, or optimiser sensitivity. How many runs to do, and how the range is built, is fixed before any model runs.
discrepantAny other difference.
not_checkableThe value cannot be produced: a named blocker in the shipped code, or (Track B) unavailable data or an underspecified method.

Fixes. Every fix records the file, the lines, what changed and why the analysis is unchanged. The allowed kinds are:

  • path;
  • package install or pin;
  • exact equivalent call;
  • script order;
  • skipping a failing statement that the claim does not use;
  • print-only statements;
  • translation.

Not allowed: writing missing analysis code, changing the model, sample or data, or switching on an option the authors describe but do not run (G-21).

Claim outcomes. Each claim reproduces, reproduces with justified fixes, or does not reproduce, as you specified.

Scoring

  • Result credit (0 or 1). The result must be identified, given the right status, and computed from the permitted inputs by admissible code, which the evaluator checks in the source and its diff. The value must also be traceable in the run's output. Where gold has a reference value, both values must round to the same printed value. So copying the printed value earns nothing, and a not_checkable earns credit only with the blocker named.
  • Paper score. The average over claims, halved if the paper outcome is wrong. It is 0 for a hard-gate violation, a timeout, a duplicate result, more than 15 claims or an invalid certificate. Failed runs stay in the denominator.
  • Headline. The mean over all scheduled targets, one per track, with intervals from a bootstrap over papers (each twin resampled with its base paper).
  • Diagnostics alongside:
    • claim recall and outcome accuracy;
    • sensitivity and specificity;
    • a status confusion matrix;
    • nondeterminism recognised;
    • fix audit;
    • twins separately;
    • baselines that label everything match, not_checkable or discrepant;
    • minutes, tokens and US$ per target.

Each stage runs in a fresh directory. Earlier stages' files are mounted read-only at fixed paths, and prompts need no filling-in. A pipeline is a short manifest naming the executor, model, options and prompt for each stage. One agent can do S1 to S3 in a single run, or each stage can use a different model. Decomposed runs feed in the gold claim list, or a frozen earlier stage, to isolate one step. Executors are small adapters for the Codex CLI, Claude Code, OpenCode, an API loop or a human, with one function signature. The run record keeps every attempt, the configuration the provider actually used, cost and a network log. In ranking runs, each stage runs in a container that can reach only package repositories and the model's API, with the provider's own web search switched off.

How the specification was agreed

Sol (GPT-6.1, high effort), round 1: "AGREE subject to the blocking changes listed". Five blocking objections:
  • labels that the evidence did not support;
  • incomplete comparison rules;
  • a headline that rewarded the status label without checking the computation;
  • a Track B design that left the authors' code reachable;
  • stage contracts too thin to swap pipelines, and isolation that was not enforced.
All five were taken up in draft 0.2. Review
Sol, round 2: two of the five objections resolved and three partly resolved. The changes Sol still required:
  • restore its conditional positions on G-07, G-15 and G-16;
  • fix the variation protocol before any model runs;
  • define the outcome for not_computed and for partial primary failure;
  • verify that the code actually computes each value;
  • apply one consistent fabrication rule.
All applied in draft 0.3, together with its non-blocking suggestions. Sol agreed with all four proposed rules. Review
Argon (Gemini 4, high), round 1: found the most practical gaps:
  • the sandbox can read the gold labels, which are also public;
  • twins could be recognised from their local path;
  • parallel runs share one R library;
  • bundles are not archived;
  • recomputed values are never compared with gold.
Two of its points were wrong against the code, and parts of its reply drew on stale context. Points and checks
Argon, round 2: run through the Antigravity command-line tool. Two delegated attempts had returned summaries written as if Argon were the spec's author, and an OpenCode attempt returned nothing. Its review was the most useful on implementation:
  • stage contracts that a second team could not build from;
  • flooding a certificate with genuine numbers to game recall;
  • shipped outputs that a model could copy from;
  • slip evidence that only the forbidden human reports contain;
  • provider-side web search bypassing the network proxy;
  • a credit rule that penalised recovering the original software version.
Adopted in draft 0.4, apart from two points it later accepted. Review
Round 3, both: "VERDICT: AGREE as drafted". Sol checked the G-22 table in the published article against the pinned output. It also confirmed that stripping outputs breaks neither G-11, whose code does not read its log, nor G-22, whose code is extracted from the HTML first. Argon marked all its round 2 points resolved.
